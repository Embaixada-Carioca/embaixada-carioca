Quick answer

What to try from Rio's cuisine?

Feijoada, picanha, codfish fritters, pastéis and caipirinha. At Embaixada Carioca, on Urca Hill, these flavors come to the table with a direct view of Sugarloaf Mountain.

Origins

Where Rio's cuisine comes from

Rio's cooking blends African, Portuguese and Indigenous heritage. Feijoada comes from the African and Brazilian tradition; codfish from Portugal; cassava, which Rio calls aipim, from Indigenous peoples.

Rio's botequins, the neighborhood bars, gave the city its snacks to share, such as pastéis and fritters, always with cold draft beer or a caipirinha.

FAQ

Questions about Rio food

What are the typical dishes of Rio?

Among the most representative are feijoada, traditionally eaten on Saturdays, codfish fritters, pastéis, picanha and açaí. To drink, caipirinha and draft beer.

Where can I eat feijoada with a view in Rio?

At Embaixada Carioca, on Urca Hill, which serves the Academia da Cachaça feijoada every day at lunch, winner of the Best Feijoada category at Veja Rio Comer & Beber 2025/2026, through a formal partnership.

What is carioca cuisine?

It is the cooking of Rio de Janeiro, with African, Portuguese and Indigenous influences: feijoada, codfish, cassava (called aipim in Rio) and tropical fruit are among its strongest legacies.

Does Embaixada Carioca have options beyond Brazilian food?

Yes. The menu also has salads, quinoa risotto, burgers, sandwiches and a kids' menu, up to 10 years old.
